Plan and research · N-of-1 prototype · October 2026

A closed loop that catches the off-aim state and interrupts it

The system does not read thoughts. It learns the body-and-brain signature that shows up when one person leaves a state they chose, detects that signature when it recurs, fires a short conditioned cue, and measures whether the cue worked.

What is measuredEEG, skin conductance, heart rhythm and motion, fused into one probability of an off-aim loop.
What is newPersonal calibration, plus a second model that learns which interruption works for this person.
Where Robbins fitsHis live-event frameworks supply the aim, the cue design and the intervention library. The sensors grade them.
First proofTrain on four days, predict the fifth. If that fails, nothing downstream matters.
Section 1

Detect the state, leave the thought alone

No biosignal carries a channel for positive or negative thought. Skin conductance rises with fear, excitement, concentration and exercise alike. Frontal alpha asymmetry, long read as a positive-versus-negative index, tracks approach versus withdrawal: anger is negative in valence and still looks like approach.

What sensors can register is that a recurring pattern of attention and arousal has started again. For one person, with their own labels, that is a tractable classification problem.

Weak claim Positive thought vs. negative thought Universal, semantic, unsupported. Fails across people and across days.
Buildable claim On-aim state vs. off-aim recurring state, for one person Operational, personal, testable on held-out days.

Technical expansion: why the sign of arousal is missing
  • EDA has two parts. The tonic level (SCL) drifts over minutes. Phasic responses (SCRs) begin roughly 1 to 3 seconds after an event and take several more seconds to recover. Both rise with any sympathetic activation.
  • Endosomatic vs. exosomatic. Skin potential records the body's own voltage. Most consumer devices are exosomatic: they apply a tiny controlled voltage and read conductance. Either works for this project.
  • Frontal alpha asymmetry is computed as ln(right alpha power) minus ln(left alpha power) at paired frontal sites such as F4 and F3. Use it as one feature among many, labelled approach versus withdrawal.
  • Consequence for the design. Valence has to come from the person's own labels. The sensors contribute "attention moved" and "activation rose", and the pairing of those two is what separates a loop from ordinary drifting.
Section 3

The Robbins layer: a state model and an intervention library

Tony Robbins' live events, Unleash the Power Within and Date With Destiny, teach a working model of state change. It has the same shape as the engineering loop: notice the pattern, interrupt it, install a replacement, repeat until it holds. Two things make it useful here.

First, his Triad says state is produced by three levers. Two of them line up with what the sensors measure. Second, his six-step conditioning method is already a closed loop, run by a coach in a room. This project swaps the coach's eyes for a classifier.

Neuro-Associative Conditioning, step for step against the loop

1Decide what you want and what stops youIdentify the blockDefine the aim and name the recurring loop. These become the labels ON_AIM and LOOP.
2Get leverageLink pain to the old pattern, pleasure to the newWrite down why the aim matters. Used at setup and inside the visualization step of the cue. Not sensed.
3Interrupt the patternBreak the state abruptlyThe detector fires the cue the moment the gate opens. The machine does the noticing the person fails to do mid-loop.
4Create an empowering alternativeGive the need a better routeThe replacement routine: breath, aim image, phrase, back to the task.
5Condition it until it holdsRepetition with emotional intensityEvery detection is one rep. The efficacy model reinforces whichever cue produced the fastest recovery.
6Test itCheck it under pressureHeld-out days, sham trials, and the count of loops per day over weeks.

How to read this section. These are coaching frameworks. They have not been validated in controlled trials, and one published analysis of Neuro-Associative Conditioning questions its scientific grounding. Here they are treated as a well-organized set of hypotheses about what interrupts a loop. The project's value is that each one gets measured.

Section 4

Architecture: two learning problems in one loop

The four states the classifier separates

ON_AIM

Focused

Working toward the chosen goal. This is the reference state every recovery is measured against.

NEUTRAL

Neutral

Relaxed, no goal-directed work. Low activation. Must not trigger a cue.

DISTRACTED

Mind-wandering

Attention drifted, little emotional charge. EEG shifts, EDA stays flat.

LOOP · the target

Activated off-aim loop

Repetitive thought with rising autonomic activation. EEG and EDA move together, with the body still.

Technical expansion: the gate, in code

The gate exists to keep false alarms rare. Three consecutive windows at a 5-second hop puts detection 15 to 30 seconds behind onset, which fits the EDA timescale above.

WINDOW      = 20 s      # feature window
HOP         = 5 s       # new estimate every 5 s
THRESHOLD   = 0.80      # P(loop), tuned per person
CONSECUTIVE = 3
REFRACTORY  = 10 min    # no second cue inside this
SHAM_RATE   = 0.25      # detections where the cue is withheld

if all(p > THRESHOLD for p in last(CONSECUTIVE)) \
   and motion_rms < MOTION_LIMIT \
   and signal_quality_ok \
   and now - last_cue > REFRACTORY:
       arm = "sham" if random() < SHAM_RATE else policy.choose(state)
       deliver(arm); log(trial); last_cue = now

The sham arm is the piece people leave out. Loops end by themselves, so without withheld-cue trials every intervention looks effective.

Phase 0

Define the aim

1 day

Decide what on-aim means for this person before any sensor goes on. The classifier can only learn a target someone has named.

  1. Write the aim as RPM

    • Result: the specific outcome the work sessions serve
    • Purpose: why it matters, in the person's words
    • Action: the task that counts as being on-aim

  2. Name the loop

    • One recurring thought pattern, described in a sentence
    • The primary question it keeps asking
    • Which of the six needs it is serving

  3. Fix the label set

    • ON_AIM, NEUTRAL, DISTRACTED, LOOP
    • One-line definition for each, written down and not changed mid-study

  4. Design and condition the anchor

    • Pick one distinctive 3-second sound used for nothing else
    • Pair it with the peak on-aim state during daily priming, starting now

Robbins tie-in. RPM, the primary question and the six needs come from Date With Destiny. Anchoring only works if the stimulus was first paired with the state, so conditioning starts weeks before the cue is used as an interrupt.

Phase 1

Rehearse on public data

Week 1 · no hardware

Build the feature pipeline and the validation harness on data that already exists, so the first personal recording goes into working code.

  1. Autonomic classifier on WESAD

    • Chest ECG, EDA, EMG, respiration, temperature and wrist BVP, with neutral, stress and amusement labels
    • Build EDA and HRV features; classify stress vs. neutral

  2. EEG classifier on SEED

    • 62-channel EEG during emotion-inducing film clips
    • Then repeat using only the 4 to 8 channels the chosen headset has

  3. Measure the generalization gap

    • Same-session accuracy vs. leave-one-subject-out vs. leave-one-session-out
    • This gap is the number the personal study has to beat
Technical expansion: features and tooling
  • EEG: log band power in theta (4–8 Hz), alpha (8–13 Hz), beta (13–30 Hz) per channel; theta/beta and alpha/theta ratios; frontal alpha asymmetry; spectral entropy; coherence or phase-locking between channel pairs.
  • EDA: tonic level and its slope; count, amplitude and summed area of phasic responses per window after tonic/phasic decomposition.
  • Cardiac: mean heart rate; RMSSD and SDNN over 60-second rolling windows.
  • Motion: RMS acceleration per window, used as a feature and as a veto.
  • Stack: Python with MNE for EEG, NeuroKit2 for EDA and HRV, scikit-learn or LightGBM for models, BrainFlow or Lab Streaming Layer for acquisition.
  • Models: gradient-boosted trees and an SVM. Leave deep networks until there are weeks of data.
GatePipeline runs end to end and reproduces a sensible baseline on both datasets. Leave-one-subject-out results are visibly worse than same-session results; if they are not, there is a leak in the split.
Phase 2

Instrument and record

Weeks 2–3 · 8+ days of data

Collect personal ground truth during real work sessions.

  1. Assemble the rig

    • EEG headset, fingertip EDA, chest-strap ECG, phone or watch for motion
    • One clock: every stream timestamped through the same recorder

  2. Self-caught labels

    • A single button: “I am in the loop I want interrupted”
    • Tap the moment it is noticed

  3. Probe-caught labels

    • Random prompts through the day: “What were you thinking about in the previous 15 seconds?”
    • Answer with one of the four labels

  4. Daily priming block

    • 10 minutes before the first session, recorded
    • Gives a clean on-aim baseline each day and one more anchor-conditioning rep
Technical expansion: label timing and data hygiene
  • Label the window before the tap. The loop was running before it was noticed. Take the 10 to 30 seconds preceding each tap as the positive example and discard the 2 seconds around the tap, which contain the motor act.
  • Probes fix the sampling bias. Self-caught taps only capture loops the person noticed. Random probes catch the unnoticed ones and supply the negative classes.
  • Target volume: at least 30 LOOP examples and 100 examples across the other labels, spread over 8 or more separate days.
  • Synchronization: Lab Streaming Layer with all devices on one host; verify alignment with a shared event such as a hand clap seen in EEG, EMG and accelerometer.
  • Quality flags: electrode contact, EEG amplitude above about 100 µV, jaw-clench and blink bursts. Flagged windows are excluded from training and block the gate at run time.
  • Session log: caffeine, sleep, time of day, sitting or standing. These shift baselines more than thoughts do.
GateEnough labelled examples on enough separate days, with under roughly 20% of windows lost to artifacts.
Phase 3

Personal model, held-out days

Week 4 · the critical test

Train on Monday to Thursday. Predict Friday, then Saturday. A model that only recognizes its own training session is a lab artifact.

  1. Baseline-normalize per day

    • Express every feature relative to that morning's priming baseline

  2. Train LOOP vs. everything else

    • Leave-one-day-out cross-validation
    • Compare fused model against EEG-only and EDA-only

  3. Score what a user would feel

    • False cues per hour at the chosen threshold
    • Share of real loops caught, and how late
Technical expansion: what a good result looks like
  • Reference point. In one published EEG mind-wandering study, within-person classification reached AUC 0.715 and across-person 0.613. Results in that range are normal for this problem. A reported 0.95 almost always means leakage between train and test windows.
  • Proposed pass mark: held-out-day AUC of 0.70 or better, and no more than 2 false cues per hour at a threshold that still catches at least half the labelled loops.
  • Leakage checks: never let overlapping windows from the same episode sit on both sides of a split; shuffle labels within day and confirm AUC falls to 0.5.
  • Ablation: if EDA and motion alone match the fused model, the EEG headset is not earning its place and the product can be a wristband.
  • Drift: plot AUC by days since training. That curve decides how often recalibration is needed.
Go / no-goPass: move to Phase 4. Fail: collect two more weeks, tighten the loop definition, or narrow the target to activation-only detection without EEG.
Phase 4

Close the loop

Weeks 5–6

Run the detector live and deliver one fixed cue, with a share of detections left untreated as controls.

  1. Real-time inference

    • New probability every 5 seconds; gate as specified in Section 4

  2. The cue, under 20 seconds

    • Anchor chord, 3 seconds
    • One physiological sigh: double inhale through the nose, long exhale
    • The phrase, said aloud or under the breath
    • Five seconds seeing the aim as done
    • Resume the task

  3. Sham trials

    • On about a quarter of detections, log the event and deliver nothing

  4. Confirm or reject each cue

    • One tap after each cue: “yes, I was in it” or “no”
    • These taps become new training labels

Robbins tie-in. The cue walks the Triad in his order. Chord and breath change physiology, the phrase changes language, the image changes focus. It is state first, then story, then strategy, compressed into twenty seconds.

Technical expansion: measuring recovery
  • Primary outcome: seconds from detection until P(loop) stays below 0.30 for three windows.
  • Secondary: skin conductance level back within the day's on-aim band; minutes until the next detection; loops per on-task hour, week over week.
  • Comparison: cue trials against sham trials, within person. With roughly 40 or more of each, a simple difference in median recovery time with a bootstrap interval is enough.
  • Blanking: skip EEG for the first few seconds after the cue. The sigh and the spoken phrase produce muscle artifacts that would otherwise look like a state change.
GateCue trials recover faster than sham trials, and the person still tolerates the system after two weeks. If the confirm rate is below about 60%, return to Phase 3.
Phase 5

Learn which interruption works

Weeks 7–10

Turn the fixed cue into a small library and let the recovery data choose among its entries.

  1. Build the intervention arms

    • Physiology: sigh only; stand and move for ten seconds
    • Focus: the replacement primary question; the aim image
    • Language: the incantation
    • Full sequence: the Phase 4 cue
    • Sham

  2. Assign adaptively

    • Each detection draws an arm; arms with faster recovery are drawn more often

  3. Add context

    • Let the choice depend on the state at detection: high vs. low activation, time of day

  4. Report

    • Recovery time by arm, loops per hour by week, false cues per hour

Robbins tie-in. Each arm isolates one corner of the Triad. The result answers a question his events assert and never measure: for this person, in this state, which lever moves first?

Technical expansion: the learning rule
  • Start with Thompson sampling over arms, reward equal to negative recovery time, capped at a ceiling. It handles small samples and keeps exploring.
  • Keep the sham arm at a fixed rate outside the bandit so the control never gets starved.
  • Contextual step: two or three context bins at most. With a few detections per day, anything richer will not converge within the study.
  • Full reinforcement learning belongs to a multi-person product with months of data. It is the long-run architecture in the research and is out of reach for one person in ten weeks.
DecisionIf one or two arms clearly beat sham and detection held up across days, there is a product hypothesis worth testing on five to ten more people.

Section 8

Risks and guardrails

  • Overclaiming. Say “detects a personal off-aim state”. Never say “detects negative thoughts”. The second claim is false and invites regulatory trouble.
  • Regression to the mean. Loops end without help. Without sham trials the system will appear to work whatever it does.
  • Interruption fatigue. A cue that fires wrongly twice in an hour gets switched off. False cues per hour is the product metric.
  • Leverage through pain. The Robbins leverage step attaches pain to the old pattern. For a loop that is already anxious, that may raise activation. Use the pull of the aim, and let the recovery data decide.
  • Scope. This is a focus and self-regulation experiment. It is not treatment for anxiety, depression or obsessive rumination, and a loop that is distressing or persistent belongs with a clinician.
  • Neural data privacy. Keep raw recordings local and encrypted. Several jurisdictions now treat neural data as a sensitive category.
  • Licensing. Muse's SDK needs a commercial agreement before anything ships; EMOTIV's raw data needs a paid plan.
