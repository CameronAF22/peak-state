# State-change frame

The voice session uses one frame: Tony Robbins' triad, plus two findings from state research that tell us how to ask. This is a prompt design, not a quotation of his books and not a clinical protocol.

## The triad

A state, in this frame, is the bundle of three things the person is doing:

1. **Physiology.** Posture, breath, movement, face, voice.
2. **Focus.** The specific object of attention.
3. **Language.** The sentence they say to themselves about what that means.

Peak State asks for one strategy in each slot. Three strategies, no more. A strategy is a move they have already used, small enough to repeat in the next two minutes.

Physiology goes first in every later session because it is the lever they can change without a song license, a photo, or a correct interpretation.

## How the research changes the questions

- **State-dependent recall.** People retrieve a state more reliably when the cues present at encoding are present again. Each strategy must name a concrete cue, not a virtue ("I get focused").
- **If-then plans.** A strategy that names the situation and the move ("If I notice the tight jaw, I exhale and drop my shoulders") is usable. A strategy that names only the desired feeling is not stored.
- **Autonomic downshift is a body instruction.** Longer exhales and a posture change are fair physiology strategies when the person already uses them. The guide may offer that shape as a candidate only after the person has failed to name their own move, and it must still be confirmed in their words. The guide does not lecture mechanism.

## What the guide is not doing

- Diagnosing stress, anxiety, or illness from Oura data.
- Teaching a Robbins seminar, listing incantations, or quoting copyrighted material.
- Adding strategies the person did not confirm.
- Running a different intervention type (visualization journey, cognitive reframe, motivational speech) when the three strategies already exist.

## Mapping onto the older anchors

The song, the scene, and the body cue from discovery can fill a strategy. They do not add slots.

| Anchor | Usual slot |
|---|---|
| Kinesthetic cue | Physiology |
| Visual scene | Focus |
| Musical moment, or the phrase they repeat | Language, unless they clearly use the song as focus |

If a slot is already filled by an anchor they confirmed, the extraction prompt asks them to state it as a strategy and keeps their wording.
